Water begins to dry up

Micro-season 55 of 72 · Oct 8 – Oct 12

Wild geese arrive from the north.

Kōgan kitaru

The first skeins of wild geese cross autumn skies, their calls announcing the deepening of the season as cold dew settles on morning fields.

In the predawn stillness, a distant honking drifts down from clouds still holding the night's chill. The geese arrive in wavering V-formations, their wings catching the first rose light as they descend toward reed-fringed wetlands. The air carries that particular clarity of mid-autumn — sharp enough to taste, cool enough to quicken the breath.

Nature notes

Wild geese from Siberia begin their annual arrival at Japan's lakes and coastal marshlands, their calls echoing across water surfaces pearled with morning dew. Cosmos flowers stand in full bloom along riverbanks, their delicate pink petals trembling in winds that carry the faint sweetness of kinmokusei beginning to fade. The rice harvest continues in earnest, leaving stubbled fields where flocks will soon forage.

Cultural note

The arrival of the wild geese has been watched for centuries as confirmation that autumn has truly settled in. In classical poetry, the kari (wild goose) appears as a messenger between worlds — linking the living to departed loved ones, summer shores to winter skies. Farmers once timed certain late plantings by the geese's first calls, trusting the birds' ancient rhythms more than any calendar.

雁の声遠き湖面に波紋かな

kari no koe / tōki komen ni / hamon kana

wild goose calls — / across the distant lake / ripples spread

On quiet evenings now, one learns to pause mid-step and look upward, listening for that traveling cry that carries winter's first whisper south.
