Micro-season 56 of 72 · Oct 13 – Oct 17

Chrysanthemums bloom.

Kiku no hana hiraku

Chrysanthemums open their layered petals across gardens and temple grounds, their sharp, green fragrance announcing autumn's deepening.

The morning air carries a clarity that makes distant mountains seem closer than they are. Chrysanthemums unfurl in gardens throughout Japan—white, gold, russet, pale violet—each bloom a small architecture of autumn. Their scent, somewhere between herb and forest floor, rises when you brush past them in the early chill.

Nature notes

Migrating geese continue their journey southward, their calls echoing across twilight skies. Spider webs catch the low morning sun, each strand jeweled with dew that evaporates by midday. Persimmon leaves begin their slow turn from green to orange, while the fruit beneath them deepens toward ripeness.

Cultural note

The ninth day of the ninth lunar month—originally around this time—was Chōyō no Sekku, the Chrysanthemum Festival, when people floated chrysanthemum petals in sake and drank to longevity. Even now, some shrines hold chrysanthemum exhibitions, and the flower remains the emblem of the imperial house, appearing on passports and official seals as a symbol of endurance and refinement.
