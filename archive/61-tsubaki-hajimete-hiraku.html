Micro-season 61 of 72 · Nov 7 – Nov 11

Camellias start to bloom.

Tsubaki hajimete hiraku

The first camellia blossoms open in temple gardens, signaling winter's true arrival as morning frost begins to silver the fallen leaves.

In the hush of early November, the first camellias unfurl — waxy petals of crimson and white against glossy dark leaves. The air carries a new sharpness now, and breath rises visible in temple courtyards at dawn. This is the threshold moment when autumn's lingering warmth finally surrenders to winter's quiet authority.

Nature notes

Wild geese settle into their winter territories along rivers and wetlands, their calls echoing across cold morning skies. The last maple leaves cling stubbornly while ginkgo trees blaze gold against increasingly bare branches. Mountain streams run clearer and colder, and the first thin ice forms at the edges of stone water basins in shadowed gardens.

Cultural note

The camellia — tsubaki — holds deep significance in Japanese culture, adorning tea ceremony spaces and winter kimono patterns. Unlike cherry blossoms that scatter petal by petal, camellias fall whole, which historically made warriors uneasy but appealed to tea masters for their honest, complete beauty. This micro-season marks the beginning of the tea ceremony's ro season, when the sunken hearth is opened and the room's atmosphere shifts to intimate winter warmth.
