Micro-season 54 of 72 · Oct 3 – Oct 7

Water begins to dry up.

Mizu hajimete karuru

Rice paddies drain to reveal cracked earth, and the air carries the mineral scent of exposed soil as autumn settles into the land.

The paddies that shimmered green all summer now stand drained, their surfaces cracking into pale mosaic patterns under the thinning October sun. Morning mist rises from irrigation channels running low, carrying that particular smell of wet clay meeting dry air. This is the breath between harvest and rest — the land exhaling its summer waters back to the sky.

Nature notes

Dragonflies still patrol the emptied fields, their shadows crossing the stubble where rice stalks were cut. Herons stand motionless at the edges of shrinking pools, fishing with patient intensity in the concentrated waters. The evening sky holds longer bands of purple and gold, and the first autumn rains have not yet come to replenish what the earth has released.

Cultural note

This micro-season marks the traditional end of rice cultivation's active phase, when farmers would hold Mizu-agari ceremonies thanking the water deities who sustained the paddies. In some regions, small offerings are still placed at irrigation gates — sake, rice, and salt — acknowledging the partnership between human labor and the flow of water that makes Japan's staple crop possible.

干潟より鷺の一歩や秋深む

Higata yori / sagi no ippo ya / aki fukamu

From the mudflat / a heron's single step — / autumn deepens

In the cracked geometry of the drained paddy, one sees the year's labor written in earth — and already, the soil begins its quiet preparation for spring.
