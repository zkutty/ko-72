Micro-season 57 of 72 · Oct 18 – Oct 22

Crickets chirp around the door.

Kirigirisu to ni ari

Crickets gather near doorways, their chirping now close and intimate as autumn deepens toward its coldest nights.

In the blue darkness before dawn, a cricket's song rises from the threshold — no longer from distant fields, but from the very stones of the genkan. The chill has driven these small musicians to seek warmth near human dwellings, and their voices carry a plaintive clarity that seems to measure the shortening days. Dew gathers heavy on morning glories that have long since closed, and the air holds the faint sweetness of decaying leaves.

Nature notes

Crickets and other autumn insects cluster in the gaps between foundation stones and beneath wooden eaves, their songs intensifying in the evening hours before falling silent well before midnight as temperatures drop. Wild persimmons begin to show orange through yellowing leaves, while cosmos flowers reach their peak bloom along roadsides. The sky takes on a particular depth of blue unique to mid-autumn — what the Japanese call 'sora no aosa' — and migrating geese can be heard passing overhead in the early morning darkness.

Cultural note

This micro-season falls within the period of 'mushi-kiki' — insect listening — a refined Heian-era pastime that persists in quieter forms today. Families might leave a window cracked to hear the crickets' song, understanding that these voices will soon fall silent. In classical poetry, the cricket at the door is a melancholy image, suggesting both intimacy and transience: the insect seeks warmth even as winter approaches to claim it.

戸に寄りて蟋蟀の声細くなる

to ni yorite / kirigirisu no koe / hosoku naru

drawing near the door — / the cricket's voice grows thin / in the deepening cold

A single cricket falls silent mid-phrase, and in the pause, you hear the first wild geese calling south through the darkness.
