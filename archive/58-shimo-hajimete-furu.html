Micro-season 58 of 72 · Oct 23 – Oct 27

First frost falls.

Shimo hajimete furu

The year's first frost silvers the edges of fallen leaves and withered grasses, marking autumn's deepening toward winter.

Before dawn, the world holds its breath. A thin rime of frost coats the browning grass, each blade transformed into a needle of white. The air carries that particular stillness of late October mornings — when breath becomes visible and the last persimmons glow like lanterns against bare branches.

Nature notes

Migrating geese continue their southward journeys, their calls echoing through pewter skies. Wild chrysanthemums bloom in ditches and along mountain paths, their bitter fragrance intensifying in the cold. The maples on higher slopes have turned fully now, while those in the valleys still hold green at their centers, creating landscapes of layered color.

Cultural note

This micro-season marks the traditional time for preparing winter clothing and bedding. Families would air out futons and bring down padded kimono from storage, performing mushi-boshi — the ritual sunning of textiles to prevent mold and insects. The first frost also signaled farmers to harvest the last of their daikon and prepare root cellars for winter storage.

In the blue hour before sunrise, each frozen blade of grass catches the first light and releases it — a field of small flames, extinguished by the warming sun.
