Micro-season 59 of 72 · Oct 28 – Nov 1

Light rains sometimes fall.

Kosame tokidoki furu

Brief, soft rains arrive without warning, leaving the late autumn air cool and the fallen leaves glistening before the first frosts come.

The rain comes in whispers now — not the insistent downpours of summer, but tentative showers that appear suddenly from pale grey skies and vanish just as quickly. The air carries a mineral coolness, the scent of wet stone and decaying leaves mingling in the stillness. Between showers, the world seems held in suspension, each droplet on a persimmon leaf catching what little light remains.

Nature notes

Migrating birds pass through in greater numbers, their calls threading through the damp air as they move southward. The last dragonflies of autumn rest motionless on browning reeds, their wings jeweled with rain. Mushrooms push up through the forest floor in these damp intervals, while the maples deepen from orange toward crimson in the mountain valleys.

Cultural note

As October draws to a close, preparations begin for Shichi-Go-San, the celebration of children aged three, five, and seven held on November 15th. Kimono shops display their finest children's attire, and families begin selecting the long chitose-ame candies symbolizing longevity. The soft rains of this micro-season are sometimes called 'shrine-washing rains,' as if the heavens themselves are preparing sacred grounds for the coming festival.

passing autumn shower — / at the eaves, a single drop / still clinging there

The rain stops, and in the silence, a crow calls once from the cryptomeria grove, its voice carrying far through the dampened air.
