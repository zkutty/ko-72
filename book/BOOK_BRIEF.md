# Kō — illustrated book brief

Status: design pilot, 24 October 2026. This is a book concept, not an approved print edition.

## Purpose and reader

A small field guide to one cycle of Japan's 72 micro-seasons. The reader can open it at the current season or follow the year in order. Keep Kō's observational voice: nature first, then food and culture. The book should reward a slow look and should not reproduce a full newsletter on every spread.

## Editorial architecture

- **Format assumption:** 5.5 × 8.5 in (140 × 216 mm), portrait. Confirm the printer and its template before final production.
- **One season per two-page spread:** a full-page illustration on the left, edited text on the right. This creates 144 season pages. Allow approximately 8 front-matter pages, 8 pages for four seasonal dividers, and 8 back-matter pages: a 168-page planning target. Page count remains provisional until typesetting.
- **First edition language:** English prose with the Japanese season name, romaji, and Japanese haiku. A complete Japanese prose edition can reuse the same illustrations and layout system after its copy is edited. Bilingual prose on every compact spread would crowd the page.
- **Text budget:** aim for 180–220 edited English words plus a haiku. Draw from the cached letter, but rewrite for sequence and space. A short food or culture observation may replace a newsletter list. Nothing is automatically approved for print because it appeared online.
- **Sequence:** open with *Risshun* (Beginning of Spring, season ID 7, 4 February), proceed through spring, summer, autumn, and winter, and wrap through IDs 1–6. Preserve canonical IDs in the manifest and cross-reference the site's January-first numbering. This spring-first order matches the four seasonal chapters and the traditional solar-year opening; it is an editorial choice for the pilot.
- **Front/back matter:** a short note on the 72-season calendar, how to read dates and regional observations, credits and image method, source notes, and an index by English/Japanese name.

## Content and source policy

`data/seasons.json` supplies canonical IDs, names, dates, and major-season labels. `data/content_cache.json` supplies draft letters; it is not a fact-check record. The current inventory is generated by `book/build_inventory.py` and must be refreshed when new letters are published. The three legacy English-only entries are IDs 21–23.

Before final book acceptance, verify botanical and animal subjects, seasonal food and cultural claims, dates and regional qualifications, Japanese text, transliteration, and haiku originality/attribution. Record direct sources and correction decisions for each season. The separate cost-bounded newsletter audit design in [ZK-442](https://linear.app/zkutty/issue/ZK-442/design-a-cost-bounded-seasonal-recipe-and-ingredient-audit) remains its own approval gate; this book work does not implement or bypass it.

## Illustration and file contract

Pilot IDs: spring 24 (peonies), summer 38 (lotus), autumn 55 (wild geese), winter 1 (water parsley). The winter pilot uses calendar metadata only until its letter exists. The four images test botanical close-ups, water, landscape, and wildlife while sharing one visual grammar.

- `book/inventory.json` is regenerated from source data and the art manifest. Never hand-edit it.
- `book/art_manifest.json` holds hand-reviewed image subject, prompt, tool provenance, path, dimensions, and status by canonical season ID. Unknown values remain `null` or `not_started`.
- Source images use `book/art/source/NN-slug.png`; print exports use `book/art/print/NN-slug.png`. Proofs live in `book/proofs/`. Do not put type into the image pixels.
- `book/build_inventory.py --check` must pass before accepting an inventory update. It checks all 72 IDs, names, source states, and any recorded art paths/dimensions against the current files.

## Decision gates

1. **Pilot brief and inventory:** source states and format decisions are explicit.
2. **Four visual pilots:** inspect them together for motif accuracy, consistent medium, palette, composition, and accidental text; approve a style before producing the other 68 images.
3. **Spread proof:** render and inspect all eight pilot pages. Mark winter prose as pending rather than inventing a letter.
4. **Complete edition:** all 72 letters exist, book copy and image subjects have documented review, final files meet the chosen printer's specifications, and a physical proof has been corrected.

## Dependencies and scope

The pilot can proceed using available cache entries. The full manuscript cannot complete until all 72 source letters are available. The full illustration set consumes the approved visual pilot but can then grow in bounded batches. Final typesetting consumes both accepted manuscript and accepted art. No changes to the newsletter send/build pipeline are part of this plan.
